# Live words on this computer come from a small streaming model; Qwen3-ASR still writes each finished line

A Meeting transcribed on this computer (#136) shows its words twice. First come the live words, which are unfinished and change as the speaker goes on. Then comes the finished line, which is saved in the Transcript and can be cited. The engine is Qwen3-ASR 0.6B with Silero VAD, through sherpa-onnx-node. Qwen3-ASR is not a streaming model. For live words, the speech host decoded the whole stretch heard so far again every 2.5 s, one decode at a time, after any finished line waiting. The first words came 4.3 s (English) and 8.1 s (Chinese) after speech started, and the live words then changed every 3 to 6 s. The cloud routes change them every 0.2 s (#2662).

Strong products show provisional words at once and replace them in place when a better result is ready. They differ in where the provisional words come from (sources checked 2026-11-07):

- **A streaming model's own partial results, then its final result.** Apple's SpeechTranscriber calls them volatile and finalized results ([WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/), [docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechtranscriber/reportingoption/volatileresults)). Handy uses Nemotron Streaming ([0.9.0](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/main/src/content/release-notes/0.9.0.md), [transcribe.cpp](https://github.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp)). Superwhisper's realtime mode uses Parakeet, streaming models only ([docs](https://superwhisper.com/docs/common-issues/realtime)).
- **Two passes.** A streaming model gives the draft, and a stronger model reads each segment again once it ends. Google's on-device RNN-T + LAS works this way ([1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10992), [2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12710)), as does FunASR's "2pass" runtime ([guide](https://github.com/modelscope/FunASR/blob/main/runtime/docs/SDK_advanced_guide_online.md)).
- **Decoding a non-streaming model again over a growing window.** WhisperKit does this ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10860)), and it is what this computer did.

## Decision

**Two passes, in the transcription process.**

- **The live words.** A streaming Zipformer transducer for Chinese and English reads each stretch of speech as it is said: `sherpa-onnx-streaming-zipformer-bilingual-zh-en-2023-02-20`, int8, Apache-2.0, 199 MB, through sherpa-onnx-node's `OnlineRecognizer`, which the app already ships.
  - A reading starts when Silero VAD hears speech. It is fed from 0.4 s before that, then every 100 ms frame as it comes.
  - The words it has so far are the stretch's partial text (`SpeechSegment.final: false`), reported whenever they change. The stretch is never decoded again while it goes on.
  - The model writes English in capitals, so its live words are shown in lower case.
- **The finished line.** Once the speaker pauses, Qwen3-ASR decodes the whole stretch, as before, and its text is the final line. Its Words to know (#1031) are unchanged, so finished lines are the same as before on the same audio.
- **Live words stay until their line comes.** If the next stretch starts first, one partial carries both stretches' words, with the first stretch's id so the final text replaces it; the partial is then reported again for what is still unfinished. The core and the window are unchanged: they already show one partial for each stream and drop it when the final with its id comes.
- **A long stretch is decoded in parts (#1031).** Qwen3-ASR, as sherpa-onnx runs it, writes at most 128 tokens a reading and holds its prompt, the audio (13 tokens a second) and what it writes in 512 tokens. Silero's 20 s limit doesn't end a stretch (past it, sherpa-onnx only looks harder for a pause), so a busy room ran to 36 s, and past about 27 s the reading stopped with nothing in sherpa-onnx's log: the end of the line was lost.
  - A stretch longer than the engine reads at once is cut at its quietest 100 ms in the last 4 s before the limit, which for Qwen3-ASR is 15 s (room for all 128 tokens; 12 s cost context). That part is decoded for its line, and the stretch goes on from the cut as the next line. A stretch that ends longer, such as one read back after Stop 20 s at a time, is cut the same way before it is decoded.
  - The streaming model's words up to the cut stay that part's until its line comes, and a new reading starts at the cut. Each part is a line with its own times; the Transcript shows the parts as one turn (#2842), and a quote runs on across a cut.
  - Qwen3-ASR's end-of-text token ends a line: it sometimes writes it after a stretch's words and runs on with a passage of its own, and nothing after it was said.
  - The app's log says when a reading reaches either limit, and when the model ran on with none of the words.
- **One thread, on the process's own thread.** sherpa-onnx has no asynchronous call for a streaming model. One thread read the FLEURS clips at 0.044 of their length; two took 0.039, for nearly three times the CPU. Qwen3-ASR keeps decoding on sherpa's own threads (`decodeAsync`), so the two passes overlap.
- **A slow computer.** The streaming model's reading time is measured against the audio's time, every stream counted, over the last minute. If it takes more than half, live words go back to decoding the speech again until no stream is open, so the next Meeting starts with them again, and the app's log records this. The same happens if the model fails while reading; if it can't load at all, the engine loads without it. Falling behind is still said as before (#462): when finished lines fall 30 s behind, the meeting bar says "Recording…" and the rest is transcribed after Stop.
- **The download.** The streaming model is part of the speech model's download: a pinned revision, each file's size and SHA-256 checked, and the same fallback sources (#1578). Its size is in the total the meeting bar shows, and the first-recording sheet says the speech models are about 1.2 GB. On an installation that already has the speech model, the streaming model downloads on its own when a Meeting starts; transcription goes on meanwhile without it, and the next Meeting uses it. Its part of a first download is optional: if it fails, the speech model is still ready, the log says why, and the next Meeting asks for it again. Its licence, with Qwen3-ASR's and Silero VAD's, ships in `notices/speech-models.txt`.
- **Memory.** Both models stay loaded while a Meeting records or is transcribed. Five minutes after the last stream finishes, the transcription process closes the engine and ends, which frees them (#2673); the next Meeting loads them again, its audio waiting meanwhile as it does while the engine starts.

## The licence and where the model comes from

- **The weights are Apache-2.0.** The model card's front matter says `license: apache-2.0` on [the upstream](https://huggingface.co/pfluo/k2fsa-zipformer-chinese-english-mixed) and on [sherpa-onnx's export](https://huggingface.co/csukuangfj/sherpa-onnx-streaming-zipformer-bilingual-zh-en-2023-02-20) the app downloads. It was trained with icefall (`pruned_transducer_stateless7_streaming`), which is [Apache-2.0](https://github.com/k2-fsa/icefall/blob/master/LICENSE). Neither model repository has a LICENSE file of its own, so the app ships the Apache-2.0 text in `notices/speech-models.txt`.
- **Caveat: where its training data came from isn't documented.** The upstream says only that it was trained on "tens of thousands hours of some internal dataset", and its training configuration names the subset `12k_hour`. Its published scores are on WenetSpeech, which is licensed CC BY 4.0 for non-commercial use only. That is a risk about the data's provenance, not a limit on the weights' licence. If a model with documented training data and the same quality appears, it should replace this one; the speech host takes any streaming reader.

## The numbers it rests on

Measured on an Apple M2 Max on the FLEURS dev clips (8 English, 8 Mandarin), fed in 100 ms frames at speaking pace through the app's own code (`eval/transcription/live.transcription.ts`). The full before-and-after table is in `eval/transcription/README.md`.

- **Before and after, the whole pipeline:** first words at a median of 3.7 s before and 1.8 s after (English) and 3.1 s before and 0.5 s after (Chinese); live words change every 0.3 s instead of every 3 to 6 s; the process's CPU time per second of audio fell from 0.59–1.19 to 0.28–0.37.
- **Finished lines are the same, word for word:** WER 11.3% (English), CER 2.6% (Chinese), 4.6% (taking turns), and the same 383 lines on the first 4 minutes of each case of the meeting set (#85).
- **The Zipformer's own words,** the live words alone: WER 42.6% (English), CER 5.2% (Chinese), 16.1% (taking turns), against 11.3%, 2.6% and 4.6% for Qwen3-ASR's finished lines. Fed alone in 100 ms frames it reads 0.04 of the audio's time, with 199 MB of download and about 220 MB of memory.
- **English first words** came at 0.47 to 6.6 s clip by clip: the model writes nothing for the first words of some English sentences, so the delay is the model's, not the pipeline's.

## Considered options

- **One streaming model for everything, with no second pass.** The finished lines would be the streaming model's. Rejected: the live words' error rates above are far above Qwen3-ASR's, and Nemotron's Chinese is 16–18% CER with stray tokens from other scripts. Finished lines are what the User cites and what checks read, so they must not get worse.
- **Nemotron 3.5 ASR streaming as the live model** ([model](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b), licence [OpenMDW-1.1](https://openmdw.ai/license/1-1/), permissive). Its English is far better than the Zipformer's (WER 12–13% on FLEURS), but it is a 682 MB download against 199 MB, takes about 0.3 of the audio's time on two threads (6 to 15 times the Zipformer's CPU, beside Qwen3-ASR's), and its Chinese is worse. The CPU it takes is what makes a slower computer fall behind (#462). It is the one to measure again if a lighter export comes, or once GPU routes are measured (#137).
- **The streaming Paraformer.** Its Chinese and memory are no better, it updates every 0.6 s, and the [FunASR model licence](https://github.com/modelscope/FunASR/blob/main/MODEL_LICENSE) that covers the upstream weights says they are "provided for reference and learning purposes only". It also has a non-disparagement clause and terms that change by themselves, which conflicts with the Apache-2.0 tag on ModelScope.
- **The multilingual streaming Zipformer (2025-02-10, eight languages).** It needs one language for each stream, so it can't follow speech that mixes Chinese and English. Its upstream repository has no licence file. (The streaming models sherpa-onnx runs: [transducer](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/pretrained_models/online-transducer/zipformer-transducer-models.html), [Paraformer](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/pretrained_models/online-paraformer/paraformer-models.html), [Nemotron](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/nemo/nemotron-streaming.html).)
- **Decoding Qwen3-ASR again more often.** Each decode reads the whole stretch so far, so the cost grows with the stretch. At 0.15–0.23 of the audio's time on four threads, decoding it every 0.5 s can't keep up on a busy computer.
- **A native engine (transcribe-cpp, Metal, Core ML).** It needs a binding we would build and sign on every platform. The bake-off in #137 weighs that.

## Consequences

- **Quality of the live words.** Chinese live words are close to the final text, about 5–7% CER. English live words are rough: about two words in five are wrong on FLEURS, with no capitals or punctuation, and English first words come within 1.5 s for most sentences, not all. The finished line replaces them about 1 to 2 s after the speaker pauses.
- **Cost.** The streaming model reads only while someone speaks, on one thread. It adds about 380 MB of memory (the model is about 220 MB of it) and 199 MB to the first download.
- **Tests.** The fake engine has an optional streaming model (`live`), and the core's tests show live words, then the line. The engine check finds `OnlineRecognizer` in the real module, loaded as the built app loads it. The packaged app's transcription spec waits for live words from the real model.
