# Conversations are the assistant's own history, kept in SQLite

Questions are asked in the assistant, one per window (#253), not in a Mind. Its history has to follow the User from one Mind to the next, be listed per Folder, be searched across every Folder, and be linked to from the Notes an Answer was added to (#300). So the core keeps **conversations** itself, in two SQLite tables beside the Minds (ADR-0008): `conversations`, one row each, and `conversation_messages`, one row per Question and per Answer, in order. A conversation is not a Mind: it is never in the sidebar, a tab or `listMinds`. The assistant's History is the only place that lists conversations.

- **What a conversation holds.** A title (the one the User typed is kept apart from the one it names itself by), its Folder or none, and its messages. A Question keeps its text, the Mind in focus when it was asked (or none), its Search scope, the model picked and the Skill forced on it. An Answer keeps its text and Citations, model, status, how the model cited, and its Tool calls (its searches).
- **Which Folder.** The Folder of the Mind its first Question was asked from; none when it was asked from Home or with no Mind in focus. Asking later from a Mind in another Folder, or moving a Mind, doesn't move it; Move to… does, through `moveToFolder` with Minds and Documents. Deleting a Folder sends its conversations to Not in a Folder, and restoring the Folder brings them back, as for Minds.
- **No empty conversations.** One is made with its first Question, in the same transaction, never before.
- **Names.** The first Question, cut by the one naming rule (`src/shared/naming.ts`), is its name at once. A short title from the model after the first Answer (#235) replaces only that name, never one the User typed.
- **Search.** Titles and messages have their own FTS5 keyword indexes, built the way Passages' are (ADR-0009: `Intl.Segmenter`, traditional Chinese read as simplified), so English and Chinese words match. A conversation matches when every word of the search is in its title or its messages, in any Folder; results list the conversations newest first, with the matching messages and the words to mark. Not one shared index, since a contentless index can't say which kind of row matched. Not `LIKE`, which matches inside words, can't fold traditional and simplified Chinese and can't use the Library's word rules.

## Answers are kept as text and a list of Citations

An Answer is stored as the Markdown the model wrote, with a marker such as `[^2]` where each Citation goes, and a JSON list of the Citations, each with its marker, Passage, Document, version, Units, Location, quote and check. Rich Blocks, as in a Mind, were rejected: they would tie the history to the editor's schema, and an Answer in the assistant is never edited in place. The User edits it once it is in a Note.

- **Editor-independent** (ADR-0003's amendment). Nothing the editor's schema adds or drops can touch a conversation, so conversations need no content schema version and no read-only guard, and a phone or a hosted version can show one without the editor.
- **It is what a Mind's Answer is made from.** The core already turns this Markdown and these Citations into an Answer's Blocks (`markdownToBlocks`, `withCitations`), so an Answer shown in the assistant or added to a Note gets the same Blocks and Citation attributes as one written in a Mind.
- **It is what search and Question context read,** and both need the text.
- **Its check holds after a restart.** Every Citation keeps the version of the Document it quotes, as in a Mind. The text of an old version stays for as long as a Citation in a Mind, or in a conversation (one in Recently deleted too), quotes it.

## Why not in a Mind's document, or as Minds of kind `chat`

- **Not in a Mind's Yjs document** (a `chats` map beside its Blocks, as #268 built it). A conversation would belong to one Mind, so it couldn't follow the User to the next without being moved between documents; listing or searching a Folder's conversations would mean opening every Mind's document; deleting or exporting a Mind would take its conversations with it.
- **Not as Minds of kind `chat`** (ADR-0003's amendment, #199). Every list of Minds, the sidebar, tabs, ⌘K and Recently deleted would have to tell the two apart, a conversation would carry editor-shaped content and a content schema version, and a CRDT buys nothing: its messages are never edited by two people at once.
- **Rows sync without a CRDT.** A Question is never changed once asked. An Answer is written by the one computer generating it and doesn't change once it ends; regenerating it writes it again in place. So a row's newest write (`updated_at`) wins. The tables follow ADR-0003's rules: random UUIDs, ISO 8601 UTC timestamps, soft deletes with `deleted_at`, and no foreign keys, as a sync may bring a message before its conversation. A message's `position` is given on the computer that adds it; if a sync brings two at one position, `created_at` and then `id` order them. Message kinds and statuses are checked in code, not by the table, so a kind a newer version writes is skipped, not refused.

## Consequences

- **This amends ADR-0003.** The `chat` kind of Mind is retired: `MIND_KINDS` is `mind` alone, `kind` stays for sheets, decks and boards, and migration 45 turns any `chat` Mind that exists into a plain Mind. A reference from one artifact to another names its artifact's kind: `{ artifactKind, artifactId, anchor }`, where a Mind's anchor is a Block (`{ kind: "block", blockId }`) and a conversation's a message (`{ kind: "message", messageId }`). One stored without `artifactKind` is a Mind's.
- **Links back.** A Note added from an Answer keeps a reference to its conversation and that Answer. `resolveReference` says whether it is there, in Recently deleted (with its name), or gone. Restored, the link works again.
- **Delete and restore.** Deleting a conversation sends it to Recently deleted for 30 days with the other kinds; restoring brings it back with every message. Deleting it permanently, by the User or 30 days later at start-up, drops its messages, their index and its titles, and keeps the row's id and `deleted_at` so a later sync can pass the deletion on. Deleting a Mind leaves its conversations alone; their Questions name the Mind as deleted.
- **Privacy.** Conversations stay on this computer. Asking sends what a Question in a Mind sends, through the same chat data flow and consent (ADR-0022); nothing new leaves the computer. They live in the data folder's database, so backing up the data folder keeps them, and they are never in usage data (ADR-0014) or the log.
- **Answers stream into the conversation** through the same Answer code as in a Mind, which writes to a Mind's document or to a conversation's rows (at most every 50 ms). The Answer events and approval requests carry `conversationId`, with `mindId` null, for an Answer in a conversation. Each write is pushed to every window as `"conversation.message"`.
- **Not built here:** the assistant itself and how it shows Answers (#253, #201), History (#258), the short title (#235), proposals across Minds (#202) and ⌘K (#230).

## Question context in a conversation

- **Earlier Questions and Answers,** the oldest left out first within the same budget as in a Mind. A Citation the model reads, in an earlier Answer or a Mind it reads, reads as its source and an id, "[Tides, p. 3 · C2]", and `cite` takes the id as a record's passage (#1800). The Answer's Citation is then that same Citation (its Document, the version it quotes (ADR-0010), its Location and its quote), checked as any other. Read as plain references, a Mind's Citations were copied into a report as text that opens nothing. Ids are given in reading order for one Answer and never stored. An id the model still writes as text is made that Citation, never matched by a name, or taken out with words that say its source couldn't be kept.
- **The Mind in focus** when the Question was asked (#544; migration 72), read as a Question in a Mind reads its Blocks, the User's selection first. A Question keeps where the User was in that Mind, and its Answer which Mind it read and its version. The Mind is read whole while it fits (#564): a quarter of a cloud model's window, at most about 50,000 tokens; a local model's own window. A longer one keeps, after the selection, its headings, the part around the cursor and the Blocks that match the conversation's Questions by keyword search. It starts the conversation's first message, so a provider's prompt cache keeps it from one Question to the next.
- **What the Tool calls did** (#893, #945). Each earlier Answer is followed by IncarnaMind's record of what its Tool calls really did, a failed one's too (`src/core/answers/toolRecord.ts`), and what became of those changes (waiting, accepted, rejected, the Mind gone), so a later Question knows what changed and in which Mind, not only what the Answer said. When the budget runs out, an Answer's text is left out before its record. A record is never summarised by a model, because that would break checked Citations.
- **Documents open on screen** (#1334) that its Search scope holds are named on one line before the Question's own message, by the composer and by Try again. They are read for that Answer only and never kept with the Question, and they sit with the Question rather than the Mind so that opening another Document leaves the cached start as it was.
- **Words the User selected in Documents** to ask about (#101; migration 105). Each is checked again where it was selected, when the Question is asked and when Try again writes its Answer again. Only the Document's own words at that place go to the model and into the row, under the Document's name, with the Passages of their pages to cite them from. A quote whose Document changed there, or was deleted, is left out and the composer is told; a deleted Document's words stay readable with the Question but reach no later Question.
- **Call help (#1319).** During a live call, actions that read or interpret what was said draw only on its Transcript: Sum up so far, What do they want?, A follow-up question, Am I leading them?, Decisions and owners so far, and Where we disagree. They do not read Library Documents, written notes, previous suggestions or selected Document words, and with no call text yet they do not substitute Library facts. Other call-help actions may read Documents in the Question's Search scope for their stated purpose (factual terms for a reply or a next step, checking a claim, or comparing the interview guide with what was covered); they distinguish those Document facts from speech, and a suggestion explains their relevance. Not covered yet therefore keeps reading and citing the guide. An ordinary Question that asks for the written notes keeps the normal Note context.
- **A Conversation does not carry work across a Folder wall (#1934).** It still follows the User from Note to Note, but not across an information barrier. Each Question records the Folder it was scoped to, and the current wall is evaluated when a later turn is read; a null from an older Question falls back to the previous derivation rather than meaning outside forever. When the current wall differs from an earlier turn's, that turn is left out of the model context, its last Answer is not offered for copying, and its Tool history is not reused. Moving a Note or turning a wall on later cannot rewrite that provenance. With no Note open, the Question's own scope decides: one walled Folder, or no wall when it spans more than one.

## A Citation keeps the version it quotes where its Answer lands (#1869, #1979)

Add to note, New Mind, and a report put in a Mind check each Citation again against the version it quotes, at the Units it cites, and keep its Passage, Units and Location. Only a Citation whose Document hasn't changed since is checked against the current version, at its cited Units; a recheck never relocates it to another matching Unit (#1979). Why: a Citation opens the version it was made from or says the Document changed, and re-pointing it as it landed did neither: someone cites what they read, and a later version may say something else around the same words. Choosing between the old and the new version is the User's, made where the Citation is.

- **A Document changed since.** The Citation lands on the version it quotes and says the Document changed (ADR-0010). Where its quote isn't at its place in the current version, it needs re-check: its marker carries ↻, and its card says the day the Document changed and shows the cited words as they changed, word by word, with two choices, each one step of Undo:
  - **Update the sentence…** moves it to the words now at its place, checked in the current version, opens them beside the Mind and selects its sentence to rewrite.
  - **Keep citing the old version** leaves it where it is, and it stops asking while the Document stays at that version (`keptOver` on the Citation, content schema version 28); a later version asks again.
  - Otherwise only an accepted Edit with AI of its sentence moves it (#93), where its quote is found in the current version, and never one the User keeps citing on the old version.
- **The quote gone.** When less than half of the quote's words are still at its place, the card says the quote is no longer in the new version and offers Keep citing the old version alone.
- **Retention.** The version stays while a Citation in a Mind (Recently deleted included) or in a conversation quotes it, as ADR-0010 says. Undoing a landing leaves the conversation's Citation keeping it; after Update the sentence…, the old version goes at the next start once nothing else quotes it.
